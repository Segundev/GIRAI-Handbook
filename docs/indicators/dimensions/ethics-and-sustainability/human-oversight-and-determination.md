Dimension: Ethics and Sustainability Indicator: Human Oversight and Determination

=== "Definition"

    * [Human oversight](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/#:~:text=The%20goal%20of%20human%20oversight,or%20implemented%20by%20the%20user.) in the context of AI refers to the practice of having humans actively [monitor, supervise, control, review, and intervene](https://ec.europa.eu/futurium/en/ai-alliance-consultation/guidelines/1.html#:~:text=Human%20oversight%20helps%20ensuring%20that,%2Dcommand%20\(HIC\)%20approach.) in the operations of an AI system to ensure it's safe, ethical, and aligned with its intended purpose. It is a critical component of responsible AI development and deployment, especially for high-risk systems. Human oversight, as defined by the [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137) can also encompass 'inclusive public oversight.'

    * The level of human involvement varies depending on the system's risk and purpose. Two common models for human oversight are: (i) [Human-in-the-loop (HITL)](https://cloud.google.com/discover/human-in-the-loop), a hands-on approach where humans are directly involved in every decision the AI makes, especially during the training and validation phases. The AI acts as an assistant, and a human must approve or reject its output before any action is taken. Examples of this are often used in healthcare, where a doctor reviews an AI's diagnosis. (ii) [Human-on-the-loop (HOTL)](https://www.credo.ai/glossary/human-on-the-loop), an approach in which humans supervise an AI system's overall performance. While not reviewing every decision, humans are prepared to intervene if the AI system acts unexpectedly or unsafely. This method is often used for highly autonomous systems that still need a human failsafe.

    * [Human determination](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.693077/full), which is often referred to as ‘human agency’, is defined as ‘the capacity of an individual to act with free will’, which is most clearly demonstrated through the choices people make. Human determination in AI allows individuals to exercise judgment, assess complex ethical considerations, and make informed decisions. This ensures AI aligns with human values and responsible use, taking into account broader societal impacts.

=== "Why measure this indicator?"

    * This indicator is a vital safeguard against the potential risks of AI systems; the principle of 'human oversight and determination' necessitates human involvement throughout the [AI lifecycle](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/what-meaningful-human-oversight-of-ai-should-look-like), from design and development to use and deployment. This approach aims to achieve several goals, including improving AI performance and safety, upholding ethical principles like legitimacy, accountability, human autonomy, and agency, and building public trust in AI. Specifically, the introduction of policies mandating human oversight and determination acts as a quality control mechanism to prevent [algorithmic harms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.05067.pdf), while also upholding human rights and dignity based on a rights-based approach to AI.

    * The [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137) requires Member States to ensure that individuals and existing legal entities hold ethical and legal responsibility for AI systems throughout their lifecycle. This broadens the understanding of human oversight to also encompass public oversight. The framework stresses that Member States must ensure individuals retain the power to decide whether to grant control to AI systems in specific situations. It underscores the paramount importance of preserving ultimate human responsibility and accountability, regardless of how much humans rely on AI. While humans might choose to depend on AI systems for efficiency, the ultimate decision to relinquish control rests with them. Consequently, AI systems can never assume or replace human responsibility and accountability for the outcomes or impact of their actions.

    * Some jurisdictions, such as the European Union, present *human-in-the-loop* and *human-in-command* as different governance mechanisms for implementing the principle of human oversight and determination. For example, the [Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/ethics-guidelines-trustworthy-ai) defines the ‘human in the loop’ as the capability for humans to monitor every stage of the AI lifecycle and assume a **supervisory role** which can intervene on a needs basis *only*. This approach carries the benefit of enabling humans to put ‘realistic trust’ into the system in a way while also preserving the quality of the system [performance](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/human-in-the-loop).

=== "What to look out for"

    This indicator measures mechanisms (frameworks and government led initiatives) countries  have taken to promote and facilitate human oversight and determination of AI systems, and to ensure a certain degree of human control in the design and operation of AI systems. Some key areas this indicator would assess include:

    - **The promotion of meaningful human control over AI systems, ensuring that AI remains a tool to assist humans rather than replacing human judgment, particularly in critical areas, where AI-enabled decisions or actions can have broad and systemic implications.**<br>
    Examples: mandate the use of AI tools for high stake applications, which have significant potential risks to lives and livelihoods, or directly impact vulnerable and at-risk communities, to require human authorization before any action is taken.

    - **The prevention of full automation in sensitive sectors where human intervention and ethical judgment are necessary, such as in law enforcement or medicine.**<br>
    Examples: (i) A ban on the development and deployment of fully autonomous weapons systems that can select and engage targets without human intervention, as their use would bypass fundamental ethical and legal principles of war. (ii) require that all parole and sentencing recommendations made by AI systems be subject to mandatory review by a judge or a human review board.

    - **The assignment of obligations to entities using AI to designate responsible individuals who can oversee, intervene in, and ultimately be accountable for the decisions made by the system.**<br>
    Examples: (i) require organizations that deploy AI in high-stakes areas, such as financial trading, to appoint a designated AI officer who is legally and ethically accountable for any market manipulation or instability caused by their automated systems. (ii) obligate companies to provide comprehensive training for all employees who interact with AI systems, ensuring they understand the system's limitations and know when human intervention is necessary.

    - **The establishment of rights for individuals to have an opportunity for human review and appeal of decisions made by an AI system, ensuring that the final say rests with a person.**<br>
    Examples: (i) grant individuals the right to have a decision made by an AI system (e.g., denial of a social security benefit) reviewed by a human caseworker who can consider the case on its merits and override the automated outcome; (ii) provide a formal and accessible process for individuals to appeal a decision made by an AI, such as a university admissions decision based on an AI-powered screening tool; (iii) establish an independent board to review AI-driven decisions that are challenged by individuals, with the power to reverse outcomes and issue recommendations for improving the AI system's fairness.

=== "Examples"

    - **Government Frameworks**<br>
    These could be laws, regulations, policies (published or draft form), and/or guidelines. They could recommend or require the government and non-state actors to promote meaningful human control or prevent full automation in sensitive sectors. For example, the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia’s 2023 [AI Ethics Principles](https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/ai-principles.pdf) emphasize two key principles: the periodic monitoring and control of AI systems to ensure their operations and functionalities align with their design, and the necessity of human oversight in these processes. It also outlines the need for designing and developing AI systems that can withstand various uncertainties is crucial, requiring both inherent oversight capabilities and intervention tools. Additionally, it highlights the need to involve all parties throughout the AI system's lifecycle is essential to ensure the system's representativeness, reliability, and relevance.

    - **Government-led Initiatives**<br>
    These could include concrete government actions, projects or programs approved or operational during the study timeframe, funded, and managed by a government entity to advance human oversight and determination. It may also include the establishment of an oversight body mandated to monitor compliance with principles related to the indicator, the creation of an expert working group to develop policies or programmes. For example, Estonia's AI governance aligns with European regulatory frameworks, including the GDPR, which emphasises human oversight. The country’s courts use '[Salme](https://investinestonia.com/introducing-salme-estonian-courts-speech-recognition-assistant/)', an AI-powered speech recognition assistant, to transcribe court proceedings. This nationwide system records hearings and creates transcripts, with operators manually identifying attendees before each session. Court clerks [provide this oversight](https://www.techandjustice.bsg.ox.ac.uk/research/estonia)  by reviewing and correcting the AI-generated text as needed. Estonia has also introduced a [semi-automated system](https://www.kohus.ee/kohtusse-poordujale/tsiviilkohtumenetlus/maksekasu-kiirmenetlus) for small claims in civil proceedings. This system uses algorithms to generate payment orders automatically, based on information provided by the involved parties. Human oversight is still [maintained](https://www.juridicainternational.eu/public/pdf/ji_2023_32_107.pdf) for critical functions like establishing jurisdiction and ensuring proper document service.

=== "Research Guidance"

    When reviewing evidence/sources, look for terms like ‘human intervention’, ‘human control’, human-centric’, ‘human-in-the-loop’, ‘human-on-the-loop’, ‘human-in-command’. In terms of government frameworks, specific reference should be made to the principle of human oversight and determination or one of the terms mentioned above. It is important to note that frameworks may provide a requirement for, or otherwise describe or emphasise the importance of, instituting some degree of human control or oversight, without detailing any specific mechanisms or minimum requirements. In terms of government-led initiatives, look for evidence of government initiatives to advance principles of human oversight and determination in the design and roll-out of AI systems, which may include the development of draft policies or the establishment of an expert working group to explore the concept of ‘human-in-the-loop’.

    #### Useful Sources

    * Existing literature on the issue of human oversight around AI/algorithmic systems, including new developments/reforms in the country and recent academic research (e.g. reports, policy briefs, news/articles, academic papers)
    * Websites of civil society/other non-governmental organisations promoting the issue of or addressing human oversight or determination in AI
    * [OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/overview) (live repository of global AI policy initiatives)

    #### Search

    * Parliamentary or government records for recent mentions of ‘human oversight and AI’, ‘autonomy and AI’, ‘human agency and AI’, ‘human-in-the-loop’,etc.
    * General google searches for ‘human oversight and AI and \[country\]’, ‘determination and AI and \[country\]’, ‘human-in-the-loop and AI and \[country\]’, (can also include terms ‘policy’, ‘framework’, ‘law’, etc.)
    * Academic search engines ([Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/), [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/), etc.) for recent papers on ‘human oversight and AI and \[country\]’, ‘determination and AI and \[country\]’, and ‘personal autonomy and AI and \[country\]’, human-in-the-loop’, etc.

    #### Consult

    * Civil society organisations dealing with human oversight and determination and related issues such as human agency, personal autonomy, etc. in the use of AI
    * Academics/researchers specialising in the integration of human supervision and oversight in the design and deployment of AI systems
    * Computer scientists developing or AI experts regarding frameworks/solutions supporting human oversight and determination
    * Businesses or corporations providing tools/resources in support of human oversight and determination of AI systems
