Dimension: Inclusion and Diversity Indicator : Cultural and Linguistic Diversity |

=== "Definitions"

    * [Cultural diversity](https://en.unesco.org/about-us/legal-affairs/unesco-universal-declaration-cultural-diversity) refers to the range of ideas, customs and social behaviors shared by different groups of people in [time](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/culture) and place, often expressed through language. It is an integral ‘source of exchange, innovation and creativity’ which promotes the achievement of ‘a more satisfactory intellectual, emotional, moral and spiritual existence’ for all citizens. According to UNESCO, ‘\[t\]he defense of cultural diversity is an [ethical imperative](https://en.unesco.org/about-us/legal-affairs/unesco-universal-declaration-cultural-diversity), inseparable from respect for human dignity’ and accordingly ‘implies a commitment to human rights and fundamental freedoms,’ including those of minorities and/or indigenous persons. Similarly, Article 3 of the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights ([ICESCR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-economic-social-and-cultural-rights)) calls on State Parties to ensure equal access ‘to the enjoyment of all economic, social and cultural rights’ set forth in the Covenant.

    * Additionally, cultural and linguistic diversity is an underpinning value of responsible AI and may be advanced through the active participation of all groups regardless of race, color, descent, gender, age, language, religion, political opinion, national origin, ethnic origin, social origin, economic or social condition of birth, or disability, or any other [ground](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000380455).

=== "Why measure this indicator?"

    * When AI is developed within specific cultural and linguistic contexts that do not adequately represent the diversity of [global cultures](https://ai-cultures.github.io/papers/Cultural_Competencies_in_Artificial_Intelligence__NeurIPS_2022_Culture_AI_Workshop.pdf) where it is deployed, it can lead to cultural biases. These biases raise ethical concerns and perpetuate prejudices. AI systems developed by people of similar cultural groups and used in diverse cultural settings, can lead to discrimination and harm for cultural minorities, including indigenous [groups](https://ai-cultures.github.io/papers/Cultural_Competencies_in_Artificial_Intelligence__NeurIPS_2022_Culture_AI_Workshop.pdf). Significantly, too, AI systems reflecting dominant cultural norms and languages may contribute to the erosion of local cultural diversity, and the suppression of minority languages

    * The UNESCO [Recommendation](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137) on the Ethics of AI urges member states to promote and preserve cultural diversity and mitigate risks of its exclusion in AI design and development. This includes examining the impact on local languages through the use of AI systems such as natural language processing (NLP) applications and to take measures to preserve ‘endangered languages, local dialects, and tonal and cultural variations’ associated with indigenous persons and their expression.

    * Assessing state measures to decolonize AI requires a focus on cultural diversity. Historically, Western society's presumed objectivity has marginalized traditional cultural values and knowledge systems across the Global Majority. When these assumptions are embedded in AI design, they can perpetuate biases, leading to discriminatory outcomes in communities where AI systems are deployed. For instance, the lack of natural language processing capabilities in local languages within AI datasets or throughout the AI lifecycle can reproduce historical harms against marginalized groups, increasing exclusion and underrepresentation. Furthermore, if AI is not developed with due regard for local cultures, risks, and needs, alongside an understanding of local value systems, it may reproduce the historical appropriation, extraction, and/or monetization of traditional cultural knowledge [systems](https://www.fastcompany.com/90356295/the-rush-toward-ethical-ai-is-leaving-many-of-us-behind).

    * To mitigate potential harms, states and AI actors should increase cultural diversity within the AI workforce, as current AI processes are often shaped by individuals with similar backgrounds and hegemonic worldviews. Additionally, investment in local AI development is crucial. Conversely, measures can be taken to leverage AI for the protection of local and indigenous cultures and knowledge systems.

=== "What to look out for"

    This indicator examines the measures countries have in place to promote and protect cultural diversity in the design, development and use of AI. Some key areas this indicator would assess include:

    - **The promotion of the development of AI systems that are representative of and responsive to a wide range of cultures and languages.**<br>
    Examples: engaging  multicultural and multilingual groups’ in AI systems’ training and development processes

    - **The prevention of the development of AI tools that perpetuate cultural stereotypes, erase linguistic diversity, or favor dominant cultures.**<br>
    Examples: mandating ethical AI development audits that particularly assess fair and equitable representation of  diverse cultural and linguistic groups

    - **The assignment of obligations to entities developing and using AI to ensure their systems are trained on diverse datasets and are adaptable to various cultural and linguistic contexts.**<br>
    Examples: requirements for using diverse and representative datasets for training AI systems, particularly for high-risk domains and active engagement of affected community members in AI development

    - **The establishment of rights to redress for individuals and communities harmed by AI systems that misrepresent or discriminate against their cultural or linguistic identities.**<br>
    Examples: enforcement of accountability frameworks for discriminatory outcomes against cultural or linguistic identities from AI systems, designation of independent oversight bodies to respond to AI-related complaints and streamline commensurate redress mechanisms

    - **The promotion of AI systems that, by design, promote and preserve cultural heritage and/or cultural minorities, including endangered languages and indigenous languages and knowledge.**<br>
    Examples: funding AI research and projects for underrepresented languages, dedicated national projects or initiatives targeted toward cultural and linguistic preservation, particularly for endangered cultures and languages.

    - **Evaluating and addressing the impact of AI systems on local cultural diversity, particularly the effects of natural language processing (NLP) applications like automated translation and voice assistants—on the nuances of human language, expression, and minority languages.**<br>
    Examples: mandates for impact assessments of AI language  technologies and commitments to address potential negative implications on underrepresented languages and communities

    - **The enhancement of cultural and linguistic diversity in the AI workforce.**<br>
    Examples: mentorship programs to support AI workers underrepresented cultural groups and linguistic communities, enforcing anti-discrimination and inclusive hiring protocols in the AI workforce.

=== "Examples"

    - **Government Frameworks**<br>
    These could be laws, regulations, policies (published or draft form), and/or guidelines. They could recommend or commit the government and non-state actors to advance cultural and linguistic diversity in the context of AI. Egypt's 2019 [National AI Strategy](https://mcit.gov.eg/Upcont/Documents/Publications_672021000_Egypt-National-AI-Strategy-English.pdf) is addressing the risk of cultural and linguistic nuance loss in AI, by developing a plan to launch five pilot projects that integrate culture into NLP. Similarly, Slovenia's 2025 [National Program](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MDP/National_Programme_for_AI_2025.pdf) to Promote the Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence highlights the importance of fostering AI development in the national language. This program recognizes that English-language dominated digitalization can threaten the use of national languages, which are crucial for cultural identity. Furthermore, the policy underscores AI's potential in safeguarding cultural heritage and archival materials.

    - **Government-led Initiatives**<br>
    These could include concrete government actions, projects or programs approved or operational during the study timeframe, funded, and managed by a government entity to protect and / or promote linguistic and cultural diversity in AI. Egypt has [launched](https://alborsaanews.com/2019/10/27/1259212) an Applied Innovation Center ([AIC](https://mcit.gov.eg/en/Applied_innovation_Center)) with the aim of: (1) building national capacity in AI; and (2) developing a series of innovative AI projects that can be used to address a range of societal issues across a range of priority sectors. Part of this involves the AIC collaborating with tech vendors to develop ‘leading-edge systems for machine translation, text to speech synthesis, and automatic speech recognition for colloquial Arabic’.

=== "Research Guidance"

    The ways in which cultural diversity may be addressed, include (a) the development of AI in locally/culturally relevant and responsive ways; (b) the empowerment of cultural individual/group actors to use AI to promote cultural diversity; (c) investment in the local development of AI and measures to promote cultural diversity in AI; and (d) implementation of meaningful processes by which impacted communities, including cultural minorities, have the agency to make decisions throughout the AI lifecycle. You may also examine the particular AI application of ‘natural language processing’ in searches that combine the term with ‘culture’, ‘representation’, ‘representativity’ and/or ‘localisation’.

    #### Useful Sources

    * Existing literature on the importance of cultural diversity in AI/accounting for local, including indigenous, cultures and knowledge systems throughout the AI lifecycle, including any measures or specific (including proposed) policies in the country addressing this issue and recent academic research (e.g. reports, policy briefs, news/articles, white papers, academic papers)
    * Websites, statements and outputs from indigenous representatives, advocacy and civil society groups, and academics/researchers engaged in work around AI/data governance and cultural diversity
    * [OECD’s AI Policy Observatory](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/overview)

    #### Search

    * Parliamentary or government records for recent mentions of ‘cultural diversity and AI’, ‘indigenous rights and AI’, ‘minority rights and AI’, etc.
    * General google searches for ‘cultural diversity and AI and \[country\]’, ‘indigenous rights and AI and \[country\]’, ‘minority rights and AI and \[country\]’, etc. (can also add terms ‘policy’, ‘framework’, ‘law’ etc.)
    * Academic search engines ([Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/), [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/), etc.) for recent papers on ‘cultural diversity and AI and \[country\]’, ‘indigenous rights and AI and \[country\]’, ‘minority rights and AI and \[country\]’, etc.
    * Alternative search terms may include ‘cultural plurality’, ‘culture and preservation’, ‘knowledge systems and preservation’ and/or ‘localisation in AI

    #### Consult

    * Indigenous and/or minority representatives involved in policy or advocacy work around AI in the country
    * Academics/researchers working on issues related to cultural diversity and representation in AI
