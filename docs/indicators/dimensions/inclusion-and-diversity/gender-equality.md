Dimension: Inclusion and Diversity Indicator : Gender Equality

=== "Definition"

    * Gender equality grants "equal rights, responsibilities, and opportunities" to all gender identities, extending beyond the traditional [male/female binary](https://www.un.org/womenwatch/osagi/conceptsandefinitions.html) to include non-binary, transgender, and intersex individuals, as well as sexual minorities. It encompasses biological sex and self-identified gender. Gender equality in AI ensures fair and unbiased treatment across all genders. This includes equitable representation in AI development and the elimination of gender-based discrimination in AI outputs and decisions.

    * Promoting gender equality in AI necessitates addressing concerns beyond bias and discrimination, encompassing issues from the [Feminist Internet Principles](https://feministinternet.org/): access, governance, economy, expression, and privacy/consent. To combat gender bias in AI, and ensure that AI can advance gender equality, government policies should advocate for greater representation of women, non-binary individuals, and other sexual minorities in AI design and production. This boosts the ability to recognize biased data and identify potential discriminatory [outcomes](https://southernvoice.org/artificial-intelligence-ai-gender-bridging-the-gap/).

=== "Why measure this indicator?"

    * A [meta-analysis](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000374174) by Harvard, Nature Machine Intelligence, the AI Ethics Lab, and UNESCO revealed few normative frameworks for gender equality in AI, as these issues are often grouped under fairness, bias, and discrimination, a finding consistent with the first edition of the Global Index on Responsible AI. AI systems, increasingly integrated into daily software impacting employment, financial services, and healthcare, risk perpetuating or amplifying existing societal discrimination. Without systematic measurement, monitoring, government actions, and frameworks, AI could systematically disadvantage women and other gender groups. Gender inequality and bias in AI may arise from several sources such as from a lack of representation in datasets, preexisting biases in the training data, a lack of diversity in the teams designing the algorithms, feature selection prioritizing male-dominated behavioural indicators, cultural assumptions and the like.

    * Research reveals that AI-driven systems and tools can perpetuate and amplify existing gender biases. For instance, UNESCO's 2019 report, [*I'd blush if I could: closing gender divides in digital skills through education*](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000367416.page=1), highlighted how AI tools reinforce gender inequalities and discrimination against women, girls, non-binary individuals, and sexual minorities. The report also pointed out that the design of assistive AI systems, such as those used in Siri and [Alexa](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/02/12/us/alexa-bots-female-voice.html), can reinforce harmful gender norms and stereotypes, including endorsing the subjugation of women in subservient roles.

    * AI's impact on the workforce shows a gender disparity, according to [LinkedIn data](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/research/future-of-work-report-ai) from the US. Men are more likely to be employed in AI-augmented roles and to retain their jobs (54% men vs. 46% women). Conversely, women are more frequently in positions susceptible to AI disruption (57% women vs. 43% men). Furthermore, LinkedIn research indicates a slight gender gap in AI adoption, with women showing more reluctance to use AI than men (34% vs. 40%). Similarly, workforce automation threatens gender equality by negatively [impacting](https://hbr.org/2019/07/will-automation-improve-work-for-women-or-make-it-worse) women's and other gender minorities' economic empowerment through job loss. A [gender gap](https://eige.europa.eu/publications/artificial-intelligence-platform-work-and-gender-equality) already exists in the AI workforce, which is mirrored in STEM job acquisition and retention for women.

=== "What to look out for"

    This indicator takes a look at the mechanisms (frameworks and government led initiatives) countries have in place to promote and protect gender equality in the design, development and use of AI tools and systems. Some key areas this indicator would assess include:

    - **The promotion of Gender Equality and representation in the AI workforce.**<br>
      Examples: adoption of hiring  targets and reporting requirements to ensure a gender balanced AI workforce across different seniority levels, gender reporting and auditing requirements, resource allocation for bridging gender gaps in the workplace.

    - **The prevention of the development and use of AI systems and tools that perpetuate gender discrimination and bias.**<br>
      Examples: mandatory bias audits for AI systems used in hiring and employee assessment tools, required human oversight to mitigate gender-based discrimination and requiring transparent and explainable AI tools to trace AI-caused gender discrimination.

    - **The assignment of obligations and responsibilities to entities developing and using AI systems to ensure Gender Equality and prevent discrimination.**<br>
        Examples: requirements for AI developers to use gender-diverse data, conduct audits and promote algorithmic transparency, and for users to explain their use of AI models as well as have consistent human oversight.

    - **The establishment of rights to redress for individuals harmed by gender inequality or discrimination perpetuated by AI systems.**<br>
        Examples: requirements of reporting and redress mechanisms as well as clear accountability measures for AI-enabled gender discrimination.

    - **Initiatives to increase women representation in the AI industry workforce.**<br>
        Examples: scholarships, mentorships and funding schemes to promote women's participation in STEM education and the AI workforce.

=== "Examples"

    - **Government Frameworks**<br>
    These could be laws, regulations, policies (published or draft form), and/or guidelines. They could recommend or require the government and  non-state actors to advance gender equality in the context of AI risks. In the USA, the [National Strategy on Gender Equity and Equality](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/National-Strategy-on-Gender-Equity-and-Equality.pdf) recognizes the exclusion of "the voices and needs of women and girls from the development and governance of" tools like AI and in response, calls for the promotion of "equity, access, and nondiscrimination in STEM fields," including AI.<br><br>
    The 2023 [National Artificial Intelligence Strategy](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1DjpQuN_DIbkR2SmtEUTCYhYjM-zCFcPv/view%20) of the Dominican Republic seeks to create an AI training program specifically for women. This initiative aims to provide learning opportunities, develop technical skills, and promote AI careers for women. The program will also feature female AI experts as role models and facilitators, encouraging diversity, nurturing talent, and advancing gender equity in the digital transformation landscape.<br>

    - **Government-led Initiatives**<br>
    These could include concrete government actions, projects or programs approved or operational during the study timeframe, funded, and managed by a government entity to protect and / or promote gender equality in the context of AI. In Canada, the Gender-Based Analysis Plus (GBA-Plus) is a tool that is used to identify and assess how different genders may be affected by policies, programs and initiatives. A review of the [Directive on Automated Decision-Making](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32592) proposed that GBA-Plus be completed when developing or modifying an automated system ([OpenGov](https://www.opengovpartnership.org/stories/three-recommendations-for-more-inclusive-and-equitable-ai-in-the-public-sector/) Partnership). Following the review, the government has acted in accordance with the requirement by applying the GBA-Plus tool in the development and modification of automated systems.

=== "Research Guidance"

    The absence of explicit references to gender equality is an important limitation to keep in mind during your research into this indicator category. For purposes of the Index, we encourage you to select evidence that (i) explicitly mention gender equality and/or (ii) address issues relating to gender equality that  should be addressed by governments.

    #### **Useful Sources**

    * Existing literature on the issue of gender equality with respect to digital technologies/platforms/AI, including on recent policy or implementation developments/reforms in the country and recent academic research (e.g. reports, policy briefs, news/articles, white papers, academic papers)
    * Case law related to the issue with respect to digital technologies/AI
    * Government initiatives, e.g. training or awareness programs to educate stakeholders, e.g. businesses/teachers/caregivers/government bodies on gender rights in the digital age/with respect to AI
    * UNESCO's 2019 Report [*I'd Blush if I Could: closing gender divides in digital skills through education*](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000367416.page=1), UNIDO's [Gender, Digital Transformation and Artificial Intelligence Report](https://www.unido.org/sites/default/files/unido-publications/2023-11/Gender%2C%20Digital%20Transformation%20and%20Artificial%20Intelligence%20Report_0.pdf) from 2023 and [OECD's AI Policy Observatory](https://oecd.ai/en/wonk/ai-national-gender-bias) on Gender Bias in AI.

    #### **Search**

    * Existing legislative frameworks on gender equality and AI
    * Government policies, including by sector and/or department related to gender equality and AI
    * Parliamentary or congressional records or documents from other oversight bodies and/or mechanisms related to gender equality and AI
    * News and articles about recent developments and reforms in gender equality and protection against discrimination and other forms of bias, especially with reference to digital technologies, platforms and AI
    * The websites of gender protection authorities, as well as unions and other labour organisations or collectives that deal with gender representation in the workforce
    * For recent academic research on gender equality and non-discrimination in AI and digital technologies in the country
    * General searches for "AI gender policy," "AI gender equality",  "AI Women", "AI LGBTIQA+".
    * Search academic search engines (e.g., Google Scholar, arXiv, ResearchGate, etc.) for recent papers on "LGBTIQA+ AI systems" \+ \[country\].

    #### **Consult**

    * Experts on gender and technology, specifically in relation to data,digital rights and AI
    * Civil society organisations specialising in gender and AI, including those representing interests of gender-nonconforming/non-binary persons and sexual minorities (LGBTIQA+)
