Dimension: Ethics and Sustainability Indicator: Environmental Impact

=== "Definition"

    * [Environmental sustainability](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/sustainability/learn/environmental-sustainability) refers to the ability to maintain an ecological balance in the planet’s natural environment and conserve natural resources to support the wellbeing of current and future generations.

    * [Sustainable AI](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349639276_Sustainable_AI_AI_for_sustainability_and_the_sustainability_of_AI#:~:text=In%20this%20paper%20I%20propose,ecological%20integrity%20and%20social%20justice.) is an evolving concept that seeks to integrate ecological integrity and social justice throughout the entire AI lifecycle, encompassing idea generation, training, implementation, and governance. This movement advocates for the development of AI systems that go beyond mere application, prioritizing: (i) Environmental stewardship through sustainable use of environmental resources for present and future generations; (ii) Economic sustainability through models that support societies; (iii) and engendering societal values. While Sustainable AI encompasses both AI for sustainability and sustainability of AI development and deployment, this indicator focuses exclusively on the latter, that is, the environmental sustainability of AI development and use.

    * This indicator is grounded in the broader conceptual framework of Sustainable AI, which is composed of two separate, but interrelated parts: (1) AI for sustainability; and (2) sustainability of AI. [AI for sustainability](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349639276_Sustainable_AI_AI_for_sustainability_and_the_sustainability_of_AI#:~:text=In%20this%20paper%20I%20propose,ecological%20integrity%20and%20social%20justice.) refers to the application of AI systems for the purpose of achieving sustainability in some manner, such as for environmental conservation or for the achievement of the UN’s Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). [Sustainability of AI](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349639276_Sustainable_AI_AI_for_sustainability_and_the_sustainability_of_AI#:~:text=In%20this%20paper%20I%20propose,ecological%20integrity%20and%20social%20justice.) refers to sustainability of developing and using AI models, such as extent of carbon footprints, impact of computational power for training algorithms, rising temperatures of cloud storage.

=== "Why measure this indicator?"

    * The development and use of AI systems pose serious threats to the environment and long-term sustainability of the planet. For example, the Information and Communications Technology (ICT) sector is [responsible](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899\(21\)00188-4?_returnURL=https%3A%2F%2Flinkinghub.elsevier.com%2Fretrieve%2Fpii%2FS2666389921001884%3Fshowall%3Dtrue) for up to 2.8% of global greenhouse gas emissions; the design and development processes of AI technology in data centres consume high amounts of both energy and [water](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/how-artificial-intelligence-helping-tackle-environmental-challenges); and the need for new computing resources and information technology (IT) infrastructure is waste-intensive. Moreover, 82.6% of globally-generated e-waste is either incinerated or deposited into landfills; e-waste is projected to [reach](https://ewastemonitor.info/gem-2020/) 75 million metric tons by 2030, the majority of which ends up in countries in the Global South.

    * In response to the environmental risks posed by AI development, the [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137) calls upon member states to incentivise measures that monitor, protect, and support the environment. This includes actions that support sustainable consumption and production patterns, ranging from infrastructure plans to innovative business models, as well as actions that facilitate access to, and use of, sustainable energy in a way that considers its social impact. Similarly, the [OECD](https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/docserver/7babf571-en.pdf?expires=1676653254&id=id&accname=guest&checksum=903BAC856767FF128448557E7B150559) recommends that policymakers tackle several key areas to address the environmental impact of AI. These include establishing measurement standards for sustainable AI, collecting data on its environmental effects, broadening the scope of environmental impacts considered, and enhancing both environmental transparency and equity. Additionally, the [Basel Convention on the Control of Transboundary Movements of Hazardous Wastes and their Disposal](http://www.basel.int/TheConvention/Overview/tabid/1271/Default.aspx), ratified by 190 countries, prohibits the export of e-waste from more economically developed nations to less developed ones.

    * While AI can also be leveraged to promote environmental protection and sustainability in other sectors (“AI for sustainability”), this indicator is limited to assessing the sustainability of AI itself.

    * Accordingly, states must not only assess, but also take measures to mitigate, harmful environmental impacts of AI by reducing the amounts of: carbon emissions generated by AI systems; computing power used to train AI models; and e-waste/unnecessary waste of natural resources. Specifically, AI actors should conduct assessments at each stage of the AI lifecycle and use sustainable power to develop infrastructure that measures and reduces the carbon footprint from training [algorithms](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43681-021-00043-6). Legislative frameworks governing e-waste should strive to reduce its production. To promote positive environmental impact, AI systems can be [leveraged](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/how-artificial-intelligence-helping-tackle-environmental-challenges) to enable more sustainable practices in other sectors and industries, such as architecture (e.g. energy-efficient design); renewable energy (e.g. optimising deployment); as well as in agriculture, textile production, and electronics (e.g. waste management and deforestation monitoring). Because responsible AI demands identifying and acting on any adverse effects and because ethical AI further demands consideration of its social impact, examination of this indicator is necessary.

=== "What to look out for"

    This indicator examines country mechanisms, such as frameworks and government-led initiatives, that are designed to advance sustainable AI. It specifically looks at efforts to mitigate the long-term adverse environmental impacts of AI. Key areas of assessment for this indicator include:

    - **The prevention of resource-intensive AI development and deployment that contributes to excessive energy consumption and e-waste.**<br>
    Examples: (i) encourage the use of smaller, more efficient AI models for everyday tasks, rather than defaulting to large, energy-intensive ones that may only offer marginal performance gains; (ii) establish a repository of pre-trained models so that researchers and developers don't have to start from scratch.

    - **The assignment of obligations to developers to measure and report on the energy usage and environmental impact of their AI models.**<br>
    Examples: (i) require AI developers to include a "carbon label" with their models, detailing the estimated energy consumption and carbon emissions generated during training and deployment; (ii) obligate organizations to conduct and publish an environmental impact assessment for any large-scale AI project before it is deployed, outlining the project's projected energy and water usage.

    - **The establishment of rights for communities and organizations to be informed about the environmental impact of large-scale AI projects and to advocate for more sustainable practices.**<br>
    Examples: (i) establish a formal process for public consultation before the construction of new data centers, allowing communities to voice concerns and advocate for the use of renewable energy sources and sustainable land use. (ii) enact legislation that gives local communities the legal right to request and receive detailed information about the energy and water consumption of nearby data centers and AI facilities.

    - **The promotion of efforts to improve the energy efficiency of data centers, including the use of renewable energy sources, advanced cooling technologies, and optimized hardware, to reduce their significant carbon footprint and water consumption.**<br> Examples: (i) Provide tax incentives or subsidies for data centers that power their operations with 100% renewable energy, such as solar or wind; (ii) offer grants or financial assistance for data centers to invest in advanced cooling technologies, such as liquid immersion cooling, which can significantly reduce energy consumption and water use compared to traditional air-conditioning systems.

=== "Examples"

    - **Government Frameworks**<br>
    These could be laws, regulations, policies (published or draft form), and/or guidelines. They could recommend or require the government and  non-state actors to advance environmental sustainability as it relates to AI. France’s [National AI Strategy](https://www.inria.fr/sites/default/files/2021-08/2021-08-09_PNRIA-Flyer_National_EN.pdf) includes [provisions](https://towardsdatascience.com/the-french-national-strategy-on-artificial-intelligence-c8c8fcfdace1) that address the environmental impact of AI in relation to the sustainability *of* AI. This strategic area calls for the design and development of more environmentally-friendly AI – including the development of more ecological AI infrastructure (e.g. the cloud industry).

    - **Government-led Initiatives**<br>
    These could include concrete government actions, projects or programs approved or operational during the study timeframe, funded, and managed by a government entity to address issues of environmental protection and sustainability.  The National AI Research Program (NAIRP) and the National Institute for Research in Digital Science and Technology (INRIA) are playing a leading role in the implementation of France’s National AI Strategy, specifically in research and innovation. Along with CNRS (National Centre for Scientific Research) and CEA (French Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission), INRIA co-leads the [National research programme Artificial intelligence (PEPR IA](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/our-research/france-2030/pepr/artificial-intelligence)), which aims to respond to the challenges of sustainability, ecological transition and technological sovereignty. One of the key [objectives](https://www.inria.fr/en/pepr-artificial-intelligence-france-2030) of the programme is to make AI more sustainable by making frugal AI, which is more energy efficient and data-efficient, more accessible. PEPR AI has [launched a call](https://www.inria.fr/en/pepr-ai-call-chairs-artificial-intelligence) for chairs to scientific excellence in AI, offering AI scientists a four-year opportunity to lead a research program and team in France. One of the key scientific priorities within the PEPR AI framework for this call is Frugal AI, focusing on optimising energy performance and resources for training and using models.
    In addition, the French Ministry of Ecological Transition partnered with the AI Action Summit, HuggingFace, Data for Good France on the [Frugal AI Challenge](https://huggingface.co/blog/frugal-ai-challenge/announcing-the-challenge-winners), which invited participants from academia and industry to submit AI models that were energy-efficient, and adhere to sustainable AI practices. The competition aimed to promote environmental sustainability in AI development, and encourage frugal AI model development by availing datasets and evaluation criteria for others to apply to their projects as they develop technological solutions in alignment with sustainable values

=== "Research Guidance"

    Evidence should specifically address environmental sustainability, aligning with the environmental aspects of the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). This distinction is important, as many government initiatives may highlight the use of AI for environmental purposes, which falls outside the scope of this indicator. Research should encompass only the sustainability *of* AI

    Additionally, government-led initiatives could involve creating an oversight body to monitor adherence to environmental protections and sustainability in AI, the creation of an expert working group to develop policies or programmes in relation to environmental protection and sustainability of AI, or the allocation of funding to promote advancement of environmental protection and sustainability of AI.

    #### Useful Sources

    * Existing literature on the environment with respect to AI, including recent academic research (e.g. reports, policy briefs, news/articles, white papers, academic papers)
    * Acts regarding national resources management/climate change with regard to AI
    * Sustainability segments/clauses of science and/or technology policies and AI
    * Institutions established to advance the national science and technology agenda, e.g. national science and technology council or research institutes in the context of AI
    * [OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/overview) (live repository of global AI policy initiatives)
    * The [Sustainability Criteria for Artificial Intelligence](https://www.ioew.de/fileadmin/user_upload/BILDER_und_Downloaddateien/Publikationen/2021/IOEW_SR_220_Nachhaltigkeitskriterien_fuer_Kuenstliche_Intelligenz.pdf) (applicable at both national and sectoral levels, especially for countries with high number of AI actors)
    * The [\#AIforGood](https://aiforgood.itu.int/) platform, for specific [insights](https://aiforgood.itu.int/event/navigating-the-intersect-of-ai-environment-and-energy-for-a-sustainable-future/) addressing AI’s environmental and energy footprint,  Search
    * Parliamentary or government records for recent mentions of ‘environment and AI’, ‘environmental impact and AI’, ‘sustainability and AI’, ‘sustainability of AI’, etc.
    * General google searches for ‘environment and AI and \[country\],’ ‘sustainability and AI and \[country\],’ ‘environmental impact and AI and \[country\],’ etc. (can also add terms ‘policy’, ‘framework’, ‘law’, ‘institute’, etc.)
    * Academic search engines ([Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/), [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/), etc.) for recent papers on ‘environment and AI and \[country\]’, ‘sustainability and AI and \[country\]’, etc.

    #### Consult

    * Experts (e.g. academics/researchers) and civil society/government-sponsored organisations focusing on the environment with respect to AI
    * Civil society organisations specialising in promoting effective and renewable energy use in AI development.
