Dimension: Ethics and Sustainability Indicator: Fairness and Non-discrimination

=== "Definition"

    * **Fairness** in the context of AI refers to the ethical principle that AI systems should operate without producing biased or discriminatory outcomes. It involves ensuring that an AI system's decisions and performance are not unfairly different across various demographic groups, such as those based on race, gender, or age. Ultimately, the goal is to create AI technologies that promote equitable treatment and avoid perpetuating societal biases.

    * **Bias** is defined as a prejudice for \- or against \- an individual or group in a way that is oftentimes [unfair](https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/bias#:~:text=Britannica%20Dictionary%20definition%20of%20BIAS,a%20strong%20liberal%2Fconservative%20bias.). Bias arises from the human tendency to classify people into groups according to certain characteristics (e.g. race, gender, nationality, class, etc.) and different levels of power, status, and resources they generally hold. These classifications can lead to judgments about individuals based on the level of power, status, and resources they are presumed to have based on these characteristics and association with particular groups.

    * **Algorithmic bias** refers to systematic AI errors causing unfair outcomes, often privileging one group. It stems from underrepresented, incomplete or flawed data reflecting historical inequalities, leading to discriminatory impacts on individuals or groups. This can result in unequal access to opportunities, resources, and information, potentially violating human rights and legitimizing discrimination.

    * **Discrimination** is treating individuals or groups differently based on age, disability, gender, race, sexuality, or other characteristics. **Unfair discrimination** is unjustifiable treatment of an individual or group, not aimed at achieving equity or advancing disadvantaged groups. Not all discrimination is unfair; some forms, like preferential treatment for women in STEM, promote equity, while others, like surveillance of political dissidents, perpetuate harm.

    * Freedom from unfair discrimination is a protected aspect of the right to equality, a core principle in international human rights laws. [Article 2](https://books.google.co.za/books/about/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights.html?id=qZNKAQAAMAAJ&printsec=frontcover&source=kp_read_button&hl=en&redir_esc=y#v=onepage&q&f=false) of the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)](https://books.google.co.za/books/about/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights.html?id=qZNKAQAAMAAJ&printsec=frontcover&source=kp_read_button&hl=en&redir_esc=y#v=onepage&q&f=false) states that everyone has the right to all freedoms and rights in the Declaration, without distinction of any kind, such as race, color, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, property, birth, or other status.

    * Key instruments include the International Covenant on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination ([ICEARD](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-convention-elimination-all-forms-racial)), ensuring equal protection against discrimination (Article 1); the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights ([ICCPR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights)), upholding civil and political rights and non-discrimination (Article 26); and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights ([ICESCR](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-economic-social-and-cultural-rights)), guaranteeing equal access to basic services, infrastructure, and scientific benefits, including digital inclusion (Article 15). Additionally, national frameworks clarify non-discrimination, fairness concepts, and remedies for rights violations.

=== "Why measure this indicator?"

    * Despite advancements in equality, embedded bias in AI systems threatens to exclude marginalized individuals further from economic, social, and political life. Algorithms trained with biased data or designed non-inclusively can lead to discriminatory outcomes. For instance, the 2018 [GenderShades](http://gendershades.org/overview.html) project by MIT’s Media Lab revealed a 34.7% error rate for dark-skinned women in facial recognition systems, compared to 0.8% for light-skinned men. This research revealed deeply rooted biases in the accuracy of facial recognition systems based on race and gender. It also underscored that algorithmic fairness is determined by a range of contextually driven factors.

    * Mitigating AI bias involves understanding its origin, who it harms and benefits, how it appears in data, how it reinforces discrimination, and what redress mechanisms can address algorithmic bias. This understanding clarifies governmental measures to leverage AI's potential while reducing bias and unfair discrimination.

=== "What to look out for"

    This indicator takes a look at the mechanisms (frameworks and government led initiatives) countries have in place to prevent and mitigate the risk of unfair discrimination posed by biases in the design, development and use of AI. Some key areas this indicator would assess include:

    - **The promotion of fair and equitable outcomes from AI systems, ensuring they don't produce biased results that disproportionately affect certain groups.**<br>
    Examples: (i) Auditing AI credit scoring for racial bias to prevent disadvantaging minority applicants. (ii) Designing AI public service systems, like healthcare scheduling, to prioritize equitable access and avoid reinforcing disparities. (iii) Ensuring AI content recommendation engines don't perpetuate gender stereotypes in job advertisements (e.g., STEM for men, nursing for women), promoting broader career options for all.

    - **The prevention of AI systems from perpetuating or amplifying existing societal biases and discrimination based on race, ethnicity, gender, religion, age, or other protected characteristics.**<br>
    Examples: (i) Annual audits of AI facial recognition systems to prevent higher error rates for darker skin tones, avoiding misidentification and wrongful arrests. (ii) AI models for criminal risk assessment must use fairness metrics to prevent disproportionately high scores for marginalized communities. (iii) Ban biased historical data, like past discriminatory hiring data, when training AI recruitment models.

    - **The assignment of obligations to developers and deployers to proactively identify, measure, and mitigate sources of bias throughout the AI lifecycle, from data collection to deployment.**<br>
    Examples: (i) Mandate bias impact assessments (BIAs) for high-risk AI (e.g., social services, law enforcement) to identify biases pre-deployment; (ii) Require developers to publish "model cards" detailing data, limitations, and bias mitigation; (iii) Implement real-time monitoring of AI systems to detect and correct emerging biases, such as in loan approvals.

    - **The establishment of rights for individuals to challenge decisions made by AI systems they believe are unfair or discriminatory, with access to meaningful redress.**<br>
    Examples: (i) Give individuals the right to an explanation that allows individuals to detect and challenge potential bias or discriminatory outcomes such as loan denials due to gender or racial discrimination.
        clear, understandable explanation for AI system decisions, such as loan application denials; (ii) Implement an accessible appeal process for AI decisions, allowing human reviewers to overturn biased or incorrect automated outcomes; (iii) Ensure that AI discrimination claims lead to appropriate remedies, such as a corrected decision, financial compensation, or other redress; (iv) Mandate regular, public reports on AI-related discrimination cases or bias  challenges, to ensure  transparency and accountability.

=== "Examples"

    - **Government Frameworks**<br>
    These could be laws, regulations, policies (published or draft form), and/or guidelines. They could recommend or require the government and  non-state actors to advance fairness and non-discrimination in the context of AI. For example, [Chile’s National Artificial Intelligence Policy](https://www.minciencia.gob.cl/uploads/filer_public/bc/38/bc389daf-4514-4306-867c-760ae7686e2c/documento_politica_ia_digital_.pdf%20) emphasizes identifying and mitigating biases in AI systems to protect fundamental rights, especially privacy, data protection, and non-arbitrary discrimination by government entities. It also proposes standards for algorithmic transparency in critical applications, aiming for responsible and ethical AI development that respects human rights and prevents discrimination.<br><br>
    Similarly, [Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC)](https://www.fsc.gov.tw/uploaddowndoc?file=news/202310171518250.pdf&filedisplay=%E9%99%84%E4%BB%B6_%E9%87%91%E8%9E%8D%E6%A5%AD%E9%81%8B%E7%94%A8%E4%BA%BA%E5%B7%A5%E6%99%BA%E6%85%A7%28AI%29%E4%B9%8B%E6%A0%B8%E5%BF%83%E5%8E%9F%E5%89%87%E8%88%87%E7%9B%B8%E9%97%9C%E6%8E%A8%E5%8B%95%E6%94%BF%E7%AD%96.pdf&flag=doc) issued "Core Principles and Relevant Promotion Policies for the Application of AI in the Financial Industry" on October 17, 2023\. These principles aim to help financial institutions leverage AI benefits while managing risks, ensuring fairness, protecting consumer rights, maintaining security, and fostering sustainable development. Emphasizing fairness and human-centric values, the six core principles guide financial institutions to minimize algorithmic bias in AI systems.

    - **Government-led Initiatives**<br>
    These could include concrete government actions, projects or programs approved or operational during the study timeframe, funded, and managed by a government entity to advance fairness and non-discrimination in the context of AI. It may also include the establishment of an oversight body mandated to monitor compliance with principles related to bias and unfair discrimination in AI, the creation of an expert working group to develop policies or programmes in relation to bias and unfair discrimination and AI, or the allocation of funding to promote advancement of bias and unfair discrimination in relation to AI.<br><br>
    For example, Uruguay's [2021-2024 Action Plan](https://www.opengovpartnership.org/members/uruguay/commitments/uy0143/) includes the establishment of an Artificial Intelligence Observatory within the State. This Observatory aims to foster the ethical, responsible, secure, and reliable application of AI technology while bolstering Uruguay's AI ecosystem. Its goals include developing recommendations, criteria, and principles for state AI usage and algorithmic transparency. This will involve incorporating input from various stakeholders and addressing risks such as bias and discrimination.

=== "Research Guidance"

    When reviewing sources, look for terms like 'algorithmic systems,' 'automated systems,' and 'algorithmic/automated decisions,' as they may indicate discussions about AI, bias, non-discrimination, or fairness, even if AI isn't explicitly mentioned. Prioritize evidence that offer measures to reduce bias, unrepresentative datasets, or discriminatory algorithmic outcomes, especially those protecting vulnerable groups, including indigenous communities.

    #### Useful Sources

    * Existing literature on the issue of bias/non-discrimination with respect to digital technologies/AI in the country, including recent academic research (e.g. reports, policy briefs, news/articles and white papers)
    * Case law related to the issue with respect to digital technologies/AI, including, the judiciary’s website (examine court decisions)
    * Websites of equality commissions and authorities and civil society organisations focused on discrimination and digital technologies/AI
    * Websites of unions/labour organisations focusing on bias/discrimination in the workplace with respect to AI
    * [OECD AI](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/overview) (live repository of global AI policy initiatives)

    #### Search

    * Parliamentary or government records for recent mentions of ‘bias and AI’, ‘non-discrimination and AI’, ‘representation and AI’, ‘equality and AI’, etc.
    * General google searches for ‘non-discrimination and AI and \[country\]’, ‘bias and AI and \[country\]’, etc. (can also add terms ‘policy’, ‘framework’, ‘law’ etc.)
    * Academic search engines ([Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/), [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/), etc.) for papers on ‘bias and AI and \[country\]’, ‘non-discrimination and AI and \[country\]’, etc.
    * Alternative searches may include the terms ‘minority rights’ and/or ‘indigenous rights’ with respect to AI/digital technologies and bias.

    #### Consult

    * Experts on equality and technology, specifically regarding AI/data/digital rights
    * Civil society organisations specialising in non-discrimination and AI, including those focused on rights protection for marginalised, including indigenous communities.
