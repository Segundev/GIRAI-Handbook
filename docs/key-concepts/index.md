# Key Concepts

## Artificial Intelligence

AI does not have a singular definition, however, for the purpose of this Handbook, you may reference definitions used by international organisations that reference key characteristics that are important for understanding these technologies and systems.

The [UNESCO Recommendations on AI Ethics](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137) describe AI systems as:

> “Information-processing technologies that integrate models and algorithms that produce a capacity to learn and inform cognitive tasks leading to outcomes such as prediction and decision-making in material and virtual environments”.

The EU AI Act defines an AI system as:

> “A machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that can, for explicit and implicit objectives, generate outputs such as predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing physical or virtual [environments](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202303/eu-ai-act-definition-of-ai-aligns-with-oecd-definition-biometric-risk-updated#:~:text=%E2%80%9C'Artificial%20intelligence%20system'%20(,influencing%20physical%20or%20virtual%20environments.%E2%80%9D)”.

More broadly, AI is defined as **_“systems that display intelligent behaviour by analysing their environment and taking actions – with some degree of autonomy – to achieve specific [goals](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=COM%3A2018%3A237%3AFIN)”._**

For the purposes of this study, evidence relating to AI will be understood in a relatively broad sense. For example, when researchers are requested to analyse frameworks related to AI, evidence accepted will include frameworks that use language that explicitly refer to ‘Artificial Intelligence’ to define their object, or those that encompass activities that fit in the above mentioned definitions, regardless of whether the specific term ‘Artificial Intelligence’ has been used. For example, if there is mention of ‘machine learning’,‘algorithmic modelling’ or ‘automated decision making’, but no mention of ‘artificial intelligence’ in a document, please consider these as evidence relating to AI since they mention AI-driven processes.

## Responsible AI

Responsible AI refers to the design, development, deployment, adoption, and oversight of artificial intelligence that is ethical, rights-respecting, inclusive, and aligned with democratic values and principles.

The Global Index on Responsible AI translates the [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137) into measurable benchmarks by evaluating government frameworks, initiatives, and civil society engagement in around 140 countries. By generating globally comparable data, the Index tracks commitments and practices that shape responsible AI. This evidence-based approach helps identify progress, gaps, and opportunities, ensuring that principles of responsible AI are translated into concrete action.

| Acronym   | Full Term                                           | Description                                                                                                                            |
